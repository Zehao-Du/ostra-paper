301   Our experiments address five questions: Q1: Does OSTRA outperform baseline policies across
302   simulation benchmarks (Section 4.1)? Q2: Do these benefits hold in real-world manipulation (Sec-
303   tion 4.2)? Q3: How do errors and computational costs in the foundation-model components affect
304   OSTRA? We study their impact on task success in Section 4.3 and their latency in Section 4.4. Q4:
305
      Which architectural designs contribute to performance? Q5: How sensitive is OSTRA to its key
      hyperparameters? The last two questions are addressed by the design ablations and hyperparameter

      analysis in Section 4.5.

      4.1   S IMULATION E XPERIMENTS
309
310   Benchmarks and Experimental Setup. We benchmark OSTRA on three manipulation suites,
311   with representative task rollouts shown in Figure 4. RLBench2, based on RLBench (James et al.,
312   2020), contains seven multi-stage tasks in our evaluation and is used to evaluate OSTRA on long-
313   horizon manipulation requiring consistent reasoning over object-state transitions. ManiSkill (Gu
314   et al., 2023) contains six contact-rich tasks covering insertion, placement, tool use, and stacking, and
315   is used to evaluate OSTRA on tabletop manipulation with strong geometric and contact constraints.
316   LIBERO (Liu et al., 2023) contains four suites—Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long—with ten tasks
317   each, and is used to evaluate OSTRA on diverse language-conditioned manipulation tasks with
318
      varying spatial, object, goal, and long-horizon requirements.
319   Across all three benchmarks, we use the official datasets and follow the official evaluation protocols.
320   The primary evaluation metric is task success rate. Detailed observation, training, and evaluation
321   settings are provided in Appendix A.
