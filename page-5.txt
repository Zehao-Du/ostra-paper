258   For future object-state prediction, the queries are constructed by repeating the latest observed
259   structural-node coordinates over the next Hk keyframes and augmenting them with learned
260   keyframe-time and node-identity embeddings. TCP and action queries are constructed from their
261   corresponding noisy diffusion variables. The map-first order preserves transition grounding before
262   the much denser visual context is introduced.
263   Staged Action Generation. As illustrated in Figure 2(c), OSTRA translates object-state transi-
264   tions into executable robot motion through the hierarchy object state → TCP subgoal → action. At
265   each diffusion step, future-object queries yield an object plan P M and predicted states Yb M . Noisy
266   TCP queries then combine ordered map-to-scene attention with sg(P M ) to produce a TCP plan P E
267   and TCP-noise estimate. Finally, noisy action queries condition on sg(P E ) to predict action noise
268   and gripper openness. TCP keyframes and actions are progressively denoised from Gaussian noise,
269   whereas future object states are directly re-predicted at every step. Position and rotation use separate
      noise schedules. Algorithm 1 summarizes the complete procedure.
