196   3.2            OST-M AP : O BJECT-S TATE T RANSITION M AP
197
198
      As illustrated in Figure 3, OST-Map represents task-relevant objects by composing parameterized
      primitives into parts and identity-consistent structural nodes. Persistent node identities preserve
199
      fine-grained structure while enabling state tracking over time.
200
201   For a task with N structural nodes, the OST-Map at time t is defined as an attributed graph Mt =
202   (Vt , Et ). Each node vt,i ∈ Vt is represented as
203                                                                           
                                             vt,i = ci , Pi , Ai , pt,i , qt,i ,                    (1)
204
205   where ci denotes its semantic identity, Pi and Ai denote its structural and affordance point sets, and
206   (pt,i , qt,i ) ∈ R3 × S3 denotes its 6-DoF pose. The semantic identity and local structural attributes
207   remain fixed within an episode, while the pose evolves as manipulation changes the object state.
208   Each edge et,ij ∈ Et stores its relation type, parameters, reference anchors, and relative pose; free-
209   relation edges connect pairs without task-specific relations. The ordered sequence Mt−To +1:t thus
210   captures object-state evolution in an identity-aligned space.
